Phengaris teleius adaptations after reintroduction

Genetic, morphological and behavioural change in a butterfly three decades after its reintroduction.

Phengaris (=Maculinea) teleius is a myrmecophilous butterfly that parasitises Myrmica spp. ant nests to complete its life cycle. The adult butterflies lay their eggs on Sanguisorba officinalis flowers, where the caterpillars live and feed during their first three weeks of life. Once they are developed enough, they drop to the ground and start mimicking ant sounds and performing specific behaviours to be carried by the ants into their nest, where they continue their life cycle. P. teleius caterpillars will live as ant parasites for almost one year until they complete their development and leave the nest as adult butterflies.

This species is considered endangered in Europe. In 1976 P. teleius went extinct in the Netherlands, and in 1990 it was successfully reintroduced. Eighty-six butterflies were taken from a Polish population and translocated to the Moerputten nature reserve. A difference of almost 30 butterfly generations between the original Polish and the reintroduced Dutch population offers a great and unique opportunity to study the local adaptations of P. teleius butterflies to their host ants and to detect ongoing co-evolution processes between parasite and host populations.

To get a better understanding of how reintroductions of parasites work, we performed a multi-level comparison of source and reintroduced populations of P. teleius by studying 1) population genetics, 2) morphology of adult butterflies and 3) behavioural, chemical and acoustical adaptations of the butterfly caterpillars to their Myrmica host ants.
